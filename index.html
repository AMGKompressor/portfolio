Purpose

Boxu is a 2D top-down stealth strategy game focused on planning, observation, and precision. The player infiltrates a guarded area, completes an objective, and reaches extraction without being detected, all while stuck in a box that restricts their vision.

Unlike action stealth games, Boxu removes combat entirely: every mistake matters, and detection results in failure. The player wins if the objective is complete and extraction is reached without capture; the player loses if caught.

Core Engine Systems

Simple animated diagrams below show how the main systems fit together: enemy AI, sneaking, and mission goals. More detailed demos are in Tech Demos.

Enemy AI state machine

Purpose: Show how the Enemy AI state machine works: Dormant until woken by sound or sight, then Investigate, Chase, or Search.

  • Dormant: enemy waits until something wakes him up
  • Investigate: a footstep or wall bump was heard nearby
  • Chase: enemy can see Suneku in his vision cone
  • Search: you escaped sight, then he checks the area and calms down

Stealth Perception Stack

Purpose: Stealth depends on what Enemy can see and hear. The green wedge is his line of sight. If Suneku steps into it with nothing blocking the view, Enemy chases. The yellow ring is Enemy’s hearing radius: if Suneku makes noise inside that area, Enemy is woken and sent to investigate.

  • Green wedge shows where Enemy is looking. Step in and he may chase.
  • Yellow ring is Enemy’s hearing radius. Noise inside it triggers investigate.
  • Red pulse is noise from Suneku (footsteps, sprinting, hitting a wall)

Boxu Individual Game Project

Boxu v0.5 Alpha (stealth game)

Purpose

Individual Game Project for COMP710: a top-down stealth game where the player (Suneku) collects a keycard, reaches the south extraction zone, and avoids capture by Enemy.

Challenge

Integrating movement, mouse-aim facing, collision, vision masking, noise propagation, and enemy AI into one coherent play loop on the course GP Framework.

Approach

Built incrementally from the Apr 13 GP framework import through tagged alpha milestones on GitHub: v0.1 (hitbox + vision), mission loop (Apr 15), v0.2 AI and audio (Apr 27–29), then v0.5 Gold polish (May 11).

Result

Playable vertical slice with win/lose states, HUD objectives, splash intro, footstep audio, and debug overlays for hitbox and hearing radius (H key).

Reflection

Boxu never fully matched the scope I pitched in the GDD. COMP710 time pressure, a monolithic game.cpp that was hard to extend, and recurring technical issues like enemy chase ignoring walls, vision cone edge cases, and audio and shader path bugs, so I shipped a strong vertical slice rather than the full stealth experience I had in mind.

That ceiling turned out to be useful. The systems that did work (mouse-aim vision, noise propagation, mission loop, OBB collision) became the foundation for cardBoardEngine, where the group project could go further: modular layout, BFS pathfinding, minimap, multiple enemy types, and a Windows hand-in build. Boxu on this portfolio is the individual proof-of-concept; the engine repo is where the idea was actually pushed.

Enemy AI State Machine

Purpose

Employers can see structured enemy AI beyond simple chase behaviour.

Challenge

Balancing wake-on-noise, vision cone detection, and search cooldown without pathfinding.

Approach

OcelotState FSM: Dormant → Investigate → Chase → Search with hearing radius and vision cone checks.

Result

Enemy responds to wall hits and line-of-sight; debug hearing overlay visualises awareness.

Reflection

Add grid or nav-mesh pathfinding so Enemy respects walls during chase.

Collision & OBB

Purpose

Shows physics-adjacent programming and debugging discipline.

Challenge

Red wire outline did not match player stop distance; AABB was too fat vs rotated sprite.

Approach

Result

Vision & Shaders

Purpose

Demonstrates graphical effects and shader pipeline usage.

Challenge

Early vision wedge was partially blind; tied to player position not only facing.

Approach

GLSL vision_mask fragment/vertex shaders dim world outside mouse-aim cone.

Result

Stealth readability improved; debug mode disables overlay for level editing.

Reflection

Unit-test shader uniforms separately from gameplay facing logic.

Stealth Audio

Purpose

Audio as a gameplay mechanic: noise radius alerts enemies.

Challenge

Wall thud spammed every frame; SDL_LoadWAV does not load MP3.

Approach

Edge-triggered wall hits; PCM thud.wav; randomised footstep clips; separate walk and sprint noise radius.

Result

Walk/sprint produce different noise pulses visible when H is enabled.

Reflection

Confirm SDL-only SFX policy with lecturer; consider FMOD if required for Gold.

Vision Wedge Shader

Purpose: In Boxu, the level is mostly hidden in darkness. A cone of light follows your mouse. That is what Suneku can “see.” This demo recreates that effect in the browser.

  • Lit floor is the area you are currently looking at
  • Dark fog covers everywhere else on the map
  • Red walls block sight. The wedge stops at the wall, not through it.
  • Yellow hint: try peeking around the corner down-left. Text only appears when you look at it.

Challenge: Early versions cut off the whole cone when any ray hit a wall.

Approach: A custom shader checks each direction separately so only the blocked slice stays dark.

Result: Stealth reads clearly. You feel blind until you aim, and corners matter.

OBB Collision Debug

Purpose: Show how Boxu knows when Suneku touches a wall. The red outline is the collision box. It must match what you see on screen.

  • Red outline is the rotated hitbox (OBB), not a simple square
  • Wall slide keeps the character flush with the wall instead of sticking or clipping

Challenge: A plain square hitbox was too wide and the debug outline did not match stop distance.

Approach: Oriented bounding boxes aligned to the sprite and each wall edge.

Result: Movement feels fair; press H in-game to toggle this debug view.

Noise Pulse Rings

Purpose: Sound is a gameplay mechanic. Enemy hearing matches Suneku’s noise: a 140 px walk radius and a 320 px sprint radius. Noise inside either ring can trigger investigate.

  • Walk (blue pulse) is quiet, with a smaller ripple (~140 px in-game)
  • Sprint (red pulse) is loud, with a larger ripple (~320 px)
  • Yellow ring flashes with sprint noise (320 px)
  • Inner blue ring flashes with walk noise (140 px)

Challenge: Wall bumps fired every frame and were too noisy.

Approach: Separate walk and sprint noise radius, and only play a thud when contact first starts.

Result: Players learn to walk quietly near the Enemy and sprint only when safe. Press H to see rings in-game.

Group Game Project

COMP710 group deliverable. The full game runs on cardBoardEngine, the shared C++ codebase I built so the group could develop and hand in on Mac and Windows.

cardBoardEngine

Standalone C++ repo, not part of Boxu. Clone AMGKompressor/cardBoardEngine only, then add the lib/ folder with SDL2, SDL2_image, and GLEW as described in the repo README.

View cardBoardEngine on GitHub

Purpose

Boxu proved the core stealth ideas but could not grow into everything I wanted within the individual project timeline. Refactoring those working systems into a standalone engine let the group game inherit the vision mask, collision, and footstep logic, then pathfinding, minimap, and full multi-enemy gameplay on top.

Evolve Boxu’s monolithic layout into a lighter, modular engine: clear modules, tunable PlayerConfig, and builds anyone on the project can run on macOS and Windows for submission.

Challenge

Boxu game.cpp had grown hard to navigate; parallel edits on player, map, and rendering were risky. The group game also needed a documented Windows path (Visual Studio 2022, Win32, runtime DLLs beside the executable).

Approach

Split shared systems into framework/, src/Player/, src/Map/, and a thin CardBoardApp loop. CMake produces two targets from the same tree:

  • cardboard, slimmer modular engine demo (CARDBOARD_BUILD_FULL_GAME=OFF)
  • tgp, full game target: SceneCardBoard, EnemyManager, NavGrid, battery pickups, ImGui UI, optional Xbox controller input

Shaders and textures copy next to the executable on post-build; Mac can use Homebrew or lib/; Windows links the x86 libraries from lib/.

Result

Movement, sprint, OBB collision, footstep noise pulses, and vision_mask lighting live in dedicated modules. The tgp build adds enemy types (Spirit, Hidden, Revenant), battery pickups, health damage, spawn zones, and map tuning via BasicMapLayout.

betaVersionV2-windows, latest shipping build

Checked out from GitHub (Jun 2026). On top of the full tgp game loop, this branch adds:

  • NavGrid + BFS pathfinding divides the level into 48 px walkable cells from the same wall geometry as player collision. A breadth-first search builds waypoint paths so enemies chase and relocate around obstacles instead of cutting through walls. Hidden enemies pick a new walkable cell after flashlight stun. Press H to draw green walkable nodes in view (debug overlay).
  • Minimap HUD, a top-right corner panel shows the whole map scaled down: wall lines from Map wire segments, your blip, and enemy dots only inside a tunable reveal radius (radar-style, default ~520 px). Tuning lives in MinimapConfig.h; ImGui (` key) adjusts reveal radius at runtime.
  • Full-game polish: vision mask respects walls, stun flee/respawn, item batteries, ImGui debug UI, and documented Windows/Mac CMake for hand-in.

Flashlight and vision

Reused Boxu’s GLSL pair, then extended it for tgp (Player_1 / PlayerConfig):

  • Ambient radius, 72 px lit bubble around the player
  • LMB toggles flashlight. Charge drains while on and recharges when off.
  • Batteries are world pickups that restore charge to 100%
  • Space (held) widens the stun cone. Enemies flee or respawn via NavGrid when caught in the beam.

Controls (tgp / betaVersionV2-windows)

WASD move · Shift sprint · LMB flashlight · Space stun beam · H debug (hitbox, footstep rings, BFS walkable nodes) · ` ImGui (minimap radius, batteries) · Esc quit

Reflection

Splitting cardboard vs tgp let me keep a clean teaching demo while we iterated on enemies and level spawns in the full game. Boxu had shown what was possible; cardBoardEngine was where those ideas could breathe under a shared deadline. BFS on a coarse grid was enough for fair chase without a full nav-mesh; the minimap gave playtesters layout awareness without spoiling distant enemies.

My programming contribution

Purpose

Boxu was my individual game. It proved the stealth loop worked, but the code was stuck in a single large game.cpp and I ran out of time to add pathfinding, a proper minimap, and more enemy behaviour. I pulled the parts that already worked into cardBoardEngine, and the group game runs on that engine instead of starting again.

For a small personal or group project, rebuilding from scratch would mean spending weeks on collision, shaders, footstep audio, and Mac/Windows builds before you touch new ideas. cardBoardEngine already covers that foundation in separate modules, so you can tune gameplay in PlayerConfig, drop in new enemies, and let more than one programmer edit the repo without everything breaking. The group build added the systems Boxu never had room for. Below is what I implemented in code.

What I contributed

  • Standalone repo: cardBoardEngine decoupled from Boxu so the group clones one project with documented Mac and Windows steps.
  • tgp / SceneCardBoard, integrated player, map, UI, items, and enemy systems in one scene loop.
  • Enemy stack: EnemyManager + three enemy types (Spirit, Hidden, Revenant) with flashlight stun flee and respawn.
  • BFS pathfinding: NavGrid marks walkable cells from map walls; enemies follow BFS waypoint paths when chasing or relocating after stun (debug walkable nodes with H).
  • Minimap HUD, corner radar of the full level with wall overlay; enemy blips only within a tunable reveal radius so distant threats stay hidden.
  • Lighting gameplay: flashlight charge, batteries, and Space stun cone carried from Boxu’s vision_mask work into tgp.

Result

The group game ships from cardBoardEngine on branch betaVersionV2-windows (tgp.exe Windows Release for hand-in; Mac builds for local iteration). It delivers more of the original design intent than Boxu could alone: fair enemy routing, HUD tools for playtesting, and room to iterate in parallel. Boxu remains the polished individual slice on this portfolio; the engine is where the project scaled.

Reflection

Extracting Boxu into modules was the right call once individual scope and technical debt collided with the group deadline. Everyone could use what already worked without re-solving collision or lighting, and I could focus new time on systems Boxu never had time for (BFS nodes, minimap, Windows CMake). Module boundaries helped once several people edited player and map logic at once.
