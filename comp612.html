Snow Scene

Procedural 2D winter scene (freeGLUT)

Purpose

Show procedural 2D graphics in C: irregular terrain, by-vertex colour and alpha blending, a layered sky, a character built from primitives, and a reusable snow particle system with front/back depth rules.

What I built

  • Irregular hill polygon with by-vertex white gradient (gradD tuning); 13 ground control points for varied silhouettes
  • Sky drawn as geometry with a five-layer gradient (not only glClearColor)
  • Procedural snowman: body, head, arms, carrot nose, eyes, and optional top hat with per-part Z ordering and radial gradients for a pseudo-3D look
  • Snow particles (up to 1,500 in the shipped build; tested far higher): spawn over time, sway, fall with gravity, fade near the ground, recycle, and wrap horizontally so flakes stay in view
  • S toggles snowfall; Q quits (ESC avoided after it caused Mac key-repeat side effects when killing the process from the terminal)
  • Extra features from the brief: hat collision on the front snow layer only (hat position shared into particle.c via hatAdjustment), wind on arrow keys, colour-coded particle HUD, diagnostic mode (green = front layer, red = behind the snowman)

Challenge

Random terrain vertices re-generated every frame made the ground look like it was spinning; stable hills fixed that. The snowman figure vanished more than once: a typo in glVertex2f, duplicate torso coordinates, and draw-order bugs (sky drawn before ground hid the body). Layering took repeated passes until ground was drawn first and face parts sat in front of the head. The HUD refused to update until I moved live stats into idle() instead of display(), and double glutSwapBuffers caused screen flashing.

Approach

Iterative Mac builds, then Visual Studio packaging. I separated ground mesh from gradient logic so tuning did not shrink the hill mesh. Particle opacity falls off toward the ground; flakes that leave the left/right edge respawn on the opposite side. Hat collision uses the same global offset as the drawn hat so the hitbox moves when the hat is raised for debugging.

Result

Snow falls in front of and behind the snowman as required, the hat deflects front-layer flakes, and the scene reads clearly on both Mac and Windows. Full day-by-day notes are in the work log PDF above.

Reflection

Most bugs were single typos or draw order, not bad math. I would lock Z-layer conventions on paper before adding optional features (hat, wind), and capture milestone screenshots so portfolio pages do not rely on memory alone.

Helicopter Scene

3D flight sandbox (OpenGL)

Purpose

Apply the 3D pipeline at scene scale: perspective camera, depth testing, lighting, texturing, fog, and grounded flight with a consistent metre-based world and matrix-only transforms (as required).

What I built

  • World scale: 1.0 GL unit = 10.0 metres (helicopter length ~10–15 m in real life, so one unit ≈ one rotor span)
  • Fuselage as a high-slice sphere (early “lemon” mesh replaced after lighting looked harsh), rear rotor on the Z axis, landing gear, wireframe toggle
  • Camera controls; I / O zoom (Mac build lacks reliable mouse-wheel zoom in GLUT)
  • GL_DEPTH_TEST so the craft sits above terrain and lighting reads correctly from below as well as above
  • Full flight input with corrected sin/cos so movement matches facing after a classic “flies left while pointing right” bug
  • Ground contact via landing gear with a buffer band (hard Y = 3.51f lock had frozen take-off; buffered collision fixed sink and stuck-on-ground issues)
  • Directional sun (straight-down angle after oblique sun looked wrong) plus helicopter spotlight with attenuation tuning; later texture pass and fog with F to compare readability
  • HUD: grounded vs flying, propeller RPM ramp before take-off; animated windmill in the scene

Challenge

Lighting stayed wrong until depth test was enabled. Movement direction disagreed with rotation until trigonometry matched OpenGL’s axes. High-speed landing overshot the Y limit; forcing Y each frame created a circular dependency until a buffer zone and debug prints verified contact at speed. Textures and fog each dimmed the scene and needed re-tuned diffuse/specular and spotlight attenuation. A corrupted ground texture was recovered from backup. Switching from GL_COLOR to GL_MATERIAL removed a liked two-tone look but matched the lighting model better.

Approach

Milestone zips from first ground plane through V2.6 (textures): camera and scale first, then rotors and zoom binds, then physics, then sun/spotlight and trees, then textures/fog/HUD. ESC closes cleanly (same lesson as the snowman project). Milestones and bugs are recorded in the work log PDF above.

Result

Controllable helicopter with believable scale, working ground contact, textured fuselage, and readable lighting with optional fog. Scene extras (HUD, windmill) show the loop beyond “fly only.”

Reflection

Depth test and scale should be day-one checks; I lost time on lighting that depth sorting solved in one line. I would prototype landing collision with max-speed tests before spotlight polish, and keep texture backups under version control.
